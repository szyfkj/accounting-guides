# The financial statements

Financial statements are the year-end summary of the books. Companies usually file them with the company registry and the tax authority. There are four main statements plus notes.

| Statement | Question it answers | Covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statement of financial position (balance sheet) | What do we own and owe? | One date (e.g. 31 December) |
| Income statement (profit and loss) | Did we make a profit? | A period (e.g. the year) |
| Statement of changes in equity | How did the owners' stake change? | A period |
| Cash flow statement | Where did cash come from and go? | A period |
| Notes | What policies and details sit behind the numbers? | — |

Many countries let small private companies use simplified standards that do not require a cash flow statement, but it is still useful for managing the business.

## Reading an income statement

| Line | Baht | What to look at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | 5,000,000 | Is it growing? Which products or customers drive it? |
| Cost of goods sold | (3,000,000) | Direct cost of what you sold |
| **Gross profit** | **2,000,000** | Gross margin = 40%. Falling margin means prices too low or costs rising |
| Selling and admin expenses | (1,400,000) | Rent, salaries, marketing, utilities |
| **Operating profit** | **600,000** | Profit from the core business |
| Finance costs | (50,000) | Interest on loans |
| **Profit before tax** | **550,000** | Starting point for corporate income tax |
| Income tax | (37,500) | Illustrative; see your country guide |
| **Net profit** | **512,500** | Added to retained earnings |

The tax line is illustrative. It assumes taxable profit equals accounting profit and uses Thailand's SME rates: 0% on the first 300,000 and 15% on the next 250,000. See [Thailand: corporate income tax](../thailand/corporate-income-tax.md).

## Four numbers worth checking every month

- **Cash in the bank vs. bills due in the next 30 days.** Profit is not cash; a profitable company can still run out of money.
- **Accounts receivable age.** Customers paying later and later is an early warning.
- **Gross margin %.** Gross profit ÷ revenue. Watch the trend.
- **Current ratio.** Current assets ÷ current liabilities. Below 1 means short-term debts exceed short-term resources.
