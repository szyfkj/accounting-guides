# Accrual vs cash basis, and depreciation

Most countries require companies to use the **accrual basis**: income is recorded when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred, not when cash moves. Tax law usually follows the same rule.

|  | Cash basis | Accrual basis (required for most companies) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Income recorded when | Money is received | The goods are delivered or the service is done |
| Expense recorded when | Money is paid | The goods or service are received |
| December invoice paid in January | January income | December income |
| Shows | Cash movement | True profit for the period |

## Common period-end adjustments

- **Accrued expense.** December electricity billed in January is still a December expense. Dr Electricity expense, Cr Accrued expenses.
- **Prepaid expense.** A 12-month insurance policy paid in October is spread over 12 months. Only 3 months are a current-year expense; the rest is a prepaid asset.
- **Unearned income.** A customer deposit for work not yet done is a liability, not income.
- **Bad debts.** Tax rules often allow a bad debt write-off only after you meet specific collection conditions, so ask your accountant before writing one off.

## Depreciation

A long-lived asset such as a vehicle or computer is not an expense on the day you buy it. Its cost is spread over the years it is used; each year's share is depreciation expense.

$$
\text{Annual depreciation (straight line)} = \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Residual value}}{\text{Useful life in years}}
$$

Example: a 90,000 baht laptop used for 3 years with no residual value gives 30,000 baht of depreciation a year.

Tax rules on depreciation and bad debts differ by country. See [Thailand: corporate income tax](../thailand/corporate-income-tax.md#tax-rules-for-depreciation-and-bad-debts).
