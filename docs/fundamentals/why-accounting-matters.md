# Why accounting matters

Accounting is the system that records every money event in a business and turns those records into reports people can trust. In most countries it is also a legal duty: companies must keep books, and the tax you pay is calculated from them. See your country guide for the exact rules.

**Bookkeeping** is the daily recording: each sale, purchase, payment and receipt is written down with its supporting document. **Accounting** is the wider job: classifying those records, adjusting them at period end, and producing financial statements.

Good books give you three things:

- **Control.** You know who owes you money, who you owe, and how much cash you really have.
- **Decisions.** You can see which products, customers or branches make a profit.
- **Compliance.** Tax authority and company registry filings are built from your books. Poor records mean wrong tax, penalties, and trouble in an audit.

A simple rule to remember: **no document, no entry.** Every record in the books must be backed by an invoice, receipt, contract or bank statement.
