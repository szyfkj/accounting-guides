# Double-entry bookkeeping: debits and credits

Every transaction touches at least two accounts, and the total debits always equal the total credits. That is what "double entry" means, and it is how the accounting equation stays in balance.

**Debit (Dr)** means the left side of an entry. **Credit (Cr)** means the right side. They do not mean "good" or "bad", and a bank's use of the words on your statement is from the bank's point of view, not yours.

## The rules

| Account type | A debit… | A credit… | Normal balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assets | increases it | decreases it | Debit |
| Expenses | increases it | decreases it | Debit |
| Liabilities | decreases it | increases it | Credit |
| Equity | decreases it | increases it | Credit |
| Income | decreases it | increases it | Credit |

A memory aid: **assets and expenses go up on the debit side; everything else goes up on the credit side.**

## Worked journal entries

Worked entries that include local taxes (VAT, withholding tax, social security) are in each country guide. See [Thailand: journal entries](../thailand/journal-entries.md).

In each entry, check that the Dr column total equals the Cr column total. If they differ, there is an error.
