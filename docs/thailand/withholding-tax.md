# Withholding Tax (WHT, ภาษีหัก ณ ที่จ่าย)

When a company pays for certain services, rent and other income, it must **deduct a percentage from the payment and send it to the Revenue Department** on the payee's behalf. It is an advance payment of the payee's income tax, not an extra tax: the payee credits it against its own CIT or personal income tax.

Withholding tax is worked out on the amount **before VAT**, and generally applies to payments of 1,000 baht or more.

## Common rates for payments to Thai companies

| Payment type | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Services, professional and contractor fees | 3% |
| Rent of property or equipment | 5% |
| Advertising | 2% |
| Transport (not public transport) | 1% |
| Interest | 1% |
| Prizes and promotional awards | 5% |
| Dividends | 10% |

Rates differ for payments to individuals and to foreign companies. Payments abroad for services, royalties and interest are generally withheld at 15%, and dividends at 10%, unless a tax treaty gives a lower rate. ([Forvis Mazars](https://forvismazars.com/th/en/insights/doing-business-in-thailand/tax/withholding-tax-in-thailand))

**e-Withholding Tax.** Paying through a bank's e-WHT system lets you withhold only 1% instead of 2–5%, and the bank files for you. The Cabinet approved extending this from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2027, but it still needs publication in the Royal Gazette, so confirm before relying on it ([Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/th/en/insights/doing-business-in-thailand/tax/thailand-extends-e-tax-and-e-donation-incentives)).

## Which form to file

| Form | Used for payments to |
| --- | --- |
| PND 1 (ภ.ง.ด.1) | Employees (salaries and wages) |
| PND 2 (ภ.ง.ด.2) | Individuals receiving dividends or interest |
| PND 3 (ภ.ง.ด.3) | Individuals (freelancers, landlords, contractors) |
| PND 53 (ภ.ง.ด.53) | Thai companies and juristic partnerships |
| PND 54 (ภ.ง.ด.54) | Foreign companies not doing business in Thailand |

File and pay by the **7th of the following month** (the 15th if e-filed).

## The 50 Tawi certificate (หนังสือรับรองการหักภาษี ณ ที่จ่าย / 50 ทวิ)

The payer must give the payee a withholding tax certificate, called "50 Tawi" after Section 50 bis of the Revenue Code. As the payee, **collect and file every certificate you receive**: it is your proof for crediting the tax against your own CIT on PND 50. A missing certificate means you may pay that tax twice.

Worked example: you pay a Thai agency 50,000 baht + 3,500 VAT for marketing services. You withhold 3% of 50,000 = 1,500 baht, transfer 52,000 baht to the agency, file PND 53 by the 7th (15th e-filed) and give the agency a 50 Tawi for 1,500 baht.
