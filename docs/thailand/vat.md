# Value Added Tax (VAT, ภาษีมูลค่าเพิ่ม)

VAT is charged at **7%** on most sales of goods and services in Thailand, and on imports. The law sets 10%, but a royal decree has cut it to 7% every year for decades; Royal Decree No. 807 keeps 7% until **30 September 2027** ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/09/thailand-reduced-7-percent-vat-rate-extended-through-september-2027.html)).

VAT is a tax on the customer that the business collects for the government. A registered business does not bear the cost itself: it pays the government only the difference between the VAT it charges and the VAT it pays.

## Who must register

- A business whose taxable revenue **exceeds 1.8 million baht a year** must register (form PP 01, ภ.พ.01) within 30 days of crossing the threshold.
- A smaller business may register voluntarily, for example to claim input VAT or because large customers prefer VAT-registered suppliers.
- Once registered, you must issue tax invoices, keep an output and input tax report, and file every month even when there is no tax to pay.

## How the monthly calculation works

$$
\text{VAT payable} = \text{Output VAT (on your sales)} - \text{Input VAT (on your purchases)}
$$

Example for one month:

|  | Amount before VAT | VAT 7% |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales to customers (output VAT) | 500,000 | 35,000 |
| Purchases from suppliers (input VAT) | 300,000 | 21,000 |
| **VAT to pay with PP 30** |  | **14,000** |

If input VAT is higher than output VAT, you can ask for a cash refund or carry the credit forward to next month.

File **PP 30 (ภ.พ.30)** and pay by the **15th of the following month** (the 23rd if you e-file).

## Input VAT you cannot claim

- VAT on an abbreviated tax invoice (the short till-receipt kind)
- VAT on an invoice missing required details, or not in the company's name and tax ID
- VAT on entertainment expenses
- VAT on buying, renting or running a passenger car of up to 10 seats
- VAT on expenses not related to the business
- VAT on an invoice more than 6 months old (claim it within 6 months of the invoice month)

## Zero-rated and exempt

| Treatment | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zero-rated (0%) | VAT is charged at 0%; you can still claim input VAT | Exports of goods; services performed in Thailand and used abroad; international transport |
| Exempt | No VAT charged; you cannot claim input VAT on related costs | Unprocessed farm produce, healthcare, education, residential rent, sale of immovable property (subject to Specific Business Tax instead), domestic land transport |

## Services from overseas

When a Thai business pays a foreign provider for a service used in Thailand (software, consulting, advertising platforms), it must self-assess 7% VAT on form **PP 36 (ภ.พ.36)** by the 7th of the following month (the 15th if e-filed). That VAT can then be claimed back as input VAT on next month's PP 30.
