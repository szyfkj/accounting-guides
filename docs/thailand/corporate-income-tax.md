# Corporate Income Tax (CIT, ภาษีเงินได้นิติบุคคล)

A Thai company pays corporate income tax of **20% of its net taxable profit**, or less if it qualifies as an SME. Tax is paid twice a year: an estimate at mid-year and the final amount after year-end.

## Rates

| Taxable profit (baht) | SME rate | Standard rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 – 300,000 | 0% | 20% |
| 300,001 – 3,000,000 | 15% | 20% |
| Over 3,000,000 | 20% | 20% |

A company qualifies for SME rates when its paid-up capital is **no more than 5 million baht** at the end of the accounting period **and** its revenue from sales and services is **no more than 30 million baht** for the year. Check both tests every year. ([Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/th/en/insights/doing-business-in-thailand/tax/corporate-income-tax-in-thailand))

## From accounting profit to taxable profit

Taxable profit starts from the profit in your financial statements, then is adjusted for items the Revenue Code treats differently. Common adjustments that **add back** (increase taxable profit):

- Expenses without a proper supporting document, or not in the company's name
- Personal or private expenses of directors and shareholders
- Fines, surcharges and the corporate income tax itself
- Entertainment above 0.3% of revenue (capped at 10 million baht)
- Donations above 2% of net profit (public charities)
- Provisions and reserves, such as a general bad debt allowance
- Depreciation above the Royal Decree 145 rates

Tax losses can be carried forward and used against profits for **5 years**.

## Tax rules for depreciation and bad debts

Section 65 of the Revenue Code requires the accrual basis for corporate income tax. For the concepts, see [Accrual vs cash basis, and depreciation](../fundamentals/accrual-and-depreciation.md).

For tax, Royal Decree No. 145 sets maximum annual rates. The common ones:

| Asset | Maximum tax rate per year |
| --- | --- |
| Buildings | 5% |
| Machinery, equipment, vehicles | 20% |
| Computers and software | 33.33% (3 years) |
| Goodwill, patents, copyrights with no fixed term | 10% |
| Land | Not depreciable |

For passenger cars of up to 10 seats, the tax-deductible cost is capped at 1,000,000 baht, and input VAT on buying or renting them cannot be reclaimed. Assets costing under about 10,000 baht are often expensed straight away as a policy choice; agree a threshold with your accountant.

Thai tax law allows a bad debt write-off only after you meet specific collection conditions (Ministerial Regulation No. 186), so ask your accountant before writing one off.

## Returns

| Form | What | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PND 51 (ภ.ง.ด.51) | Half-year estimate of the year's tax; pay half of the estimated annual tax | Within 2 months after the first 6 months of the accounting year |
| PND 50 (ภ.ง.ด.50) | Annual return with audited financial statements; pay the balance after crediting PND 51 and withholding tax suffered | Within 150 days after year-end |

For a December year-end company, PND 51 is due by the end of August and PND 50 by the end of May. E-filing adds 8 days.

If the PND 51 estimate is more than 25% below the actual tax and there is no reasonable cause, a 20% surcharge applies to the shortfall. Estimate honestly.
