# The Thai legal framework for bookkeeping

Two agencies set most of the rules. The **Department of Business Development (DBD, กรมพัฒนาธุรกิจการค้า)** under the Ministry of Commerce oversees bookkeeping and financial statements under the Accounting Act B.E. 2543 (2000). The **Revenue Department (RD, กรมสรรพากร)** under the Ministry of Finance collects taxes under the Revenue Code.

## Business types and what they must do

| Form | Pays tax as | Must keep books under the Accounting Act | Audit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sole proprietor | Personal income tax | No (simple income and expense records only; a daily report if VAT-registered) | No |
| Ordinary (unregistered) partnership | Personal income tax | No | No |
| Registered ordinary or limited partnership | Corporate income tax | Yes | CPA, or a Tax Auditor (TA) if capital ≤ 5M, assets ≤ 30M and revenue ≤ 30M baht |
| Private limited company (บริษัทจำกัด) | Corporate income tax | Yes | Certified Public Accountant (CPA) every year |
| Public limited company | Corporate income tax | Yes | CPA approved by the SEC if listed |
| Foreign company branch | Corporate income tax | Yes | CPA |

## Accounting standards

Thai financial statements follow Thai Financial Reporting Standards set by the Federation of Accounting Professions (TFAC):

- **Full TFRS** for publicly accountable entities: listed companies, banks, insurers and similar.
- **TFRS for NPAEs** (non-publicly accountable entities) for most private SMEs. It is simpler: no cash flow statement required, cost-based measurement, fewer disclosures.

## Key duties of a company

- **Appoint a qualified bookkeeper (ผู้ทำบัญชี).** The bookkeeper must be registered with the DBD and meet minimum education rules. An in-house employee or an accounting firm can fill the role, but the directors remain responsible for the books.
- **Keep the books up to date** and at the registered office, or another place notified to the DBD.
- **Close the books once a year,** every 12 months.
- **Have the annual financial statements audited,** then approved at the annual general meeting (AGM) within 4 months of year-end.
- **File with the DBD** through DBD e-Filing: the audited statements (ส.บ.ช.3) within 1 month of the AGM, and the shareholder list (บอจ.5) within 14 days of the AGM.
- **Keep records and documents for at least 5 years** from the year-end; officials can extend this to 7 years.

Missing a DBD filing brings fines for both the company and its directors, so treat these deadlines as seriously as tax deadlines.
